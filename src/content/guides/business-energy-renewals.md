## How business energy contracts work

Unlike household energy, business electricity and gas aren't covered by Ofgem's price cap. Most businesses are on a fixed-term contract — often one to three years — at rates agreed when the contract was signed.

That fixed term is the part that protects you. What happens when it ends is where businesses most often lose money.

## What happens when a contract ends

If you haven't agreed a new contract by the time your current one ends, your supplier will usually do one of two things:

- **Roll you onto a new contract automatically.** Many contracts renew on the supplier's terms unless you give notice within a set window, and the new rates are rarely the most competitive you could have had.
- **Move you onto out-of-contract rates.** These are typically much higher than fixed contract rates and can change at short notice.

If you move into premises and start using energy without agreeing a contract, you'll be on **deemed rates**, which are usually expensive too.

Either way, the costly outcome is the one nobody actually chose.

## When to start

Suppliers usually let businesses agree a new contract well before the current one ends — often several months ahead. As a rule of thumb, **start looking around six months before your end date**. That leaves time to compare the market properly, make sure you're inside any notice window, and avoid a rushed decision if prices move.

If you don't know your end date, it's on your contract paperwork, or your supplier can tell you. It's the first thing we check.

## What to have ready

- **A recent bill for each supply.** For electricity it shows your **MPAN** (a long supply number, usually in a box beginning with an "S"); for gas, your **MPRN**.
- **Your contract end date**, if you know it.
- **Roughly how much energy you use a year**, in kWh — usually shown on your bill or supplier account.
- **Anything that's about to change**: moving premises, adding a site, or new equipment that uses a lot of power.

## Small businesses have extra protections

If you're a small business — a "microbusiness" under Ofgem's rules, which broadly means fewer than 10 employees or relatively low energy use — suppliers have to follow extra rules around renewals, and must show you how much any broker is being paid. If anything about your renewal looks unclear, ask before you agree to it.
