## It depends where your business is

- **England:** since April 2017, businesses, charities and public-sector organisations can choose their water retailer.
- **Scotland:** business customers have been able to choose since 2008.
- **Wales:** most businesses **can't** switch. Only sites that use more than 50 million litres a year can choose a different retailer.
- **Northern Ireland:** there's no competitive water retail market.

Households can't switch water supplier anywhere in the UK.

Strictly, which rules apply depends on whether your water company operates mainly in England or mainly in Wales — so a few areas near the border follow the other country's rules. If you're not sure, we can check for you.

## What switching changes — and what it doesn't

Your **retailer** handles your bills, meter readings and customer service. Your regional water company — the **wholesaler** — still owns the pipes, supplies the water, and deals with network leaks and water quality.

So switching can change your price, your billing and the service you get. It doesn't change the water itself, the pipes, or who fixes a burst main in the street.

## Why it can be worth a review

- **Retailers compete** on price, discounts and service.
- **Multi-site businesses** can often bring every site onto one bill with one point of contact.
- **A review can catch problems that cost money whoever supplies you** — estimated bills, meters that aren't being read, or charges that don't reflect how your site actually uses water. For example, some businesses pay sewerage charges on water that never reaches the sewer, and may be able to claim an allowance.
- **Unusually high usage** can be a sign of a leak on your side of the meter, which is worth finding quickly.

## What to have ready

- **A recent water bill for each site.** It shows your supply point ID (SPID).
- **Whether you're on a fixed-term contract** with your current retailer, and when it ends.
